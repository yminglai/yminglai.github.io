What

Can an LLM judge which research approach will win — before the results exist?

Bole shows judges only the ex-ante description of competing research attempts. Reality — many systems attempting the same task under the same budget — settles who was right. No committee votes, no citation proxies: outcomes resolve every item.

How

  1. Blinded pairs. Two attempts at the same ML research task, described up to the decision point — identity, scores, and results stripped.
  2. Judges predict. Models (or you) pick the attempt that achieved the higher final score, with a confidence.
  3. Reality resolves. Scoring uses gap-stratified accuracy, calibration, and selection regret — not raw win rate, which rewards whoever controls the item pool.

Findings so far

  • Most of the taste is an audacity prior. A one-line heuristic — pick the bolder attempt — scores 77.9% on clear-cut pairs, beating every frontier judge. Where boldness ties, judges keep a real but thin edge (+10–13 pts): that residue is the taste worth measuring.
  • One Bo Le for the whole field. Across 8 judges from 5 model families, 4.5% of items are missed by every judge — ≈135× what independent errors would predict (audited: bank defects excluded, only 5 of 90 shared misses are pure noise). Shared training begets shared taste, and shared blind spots.
  • Taste is worth real compute. The best judge, picking 1 of 10 real attempts, captures 75% of the oracle–random gap. The worst judge is below random: an unevaluated research preference model can be worse than a coin.
  • Judges obey evidence, they don't weigh it. When exploration evidence points the wrong way (28% of the time), accuracy collapses to 23–45%. Trust is a distinct, measurable sub-skill.
  • The signal is shallow but real. A 350-character gist captures most of the description signal; process evidence adds up to +18 points; author identity adds almost nothing.

Next · AntePost — a benchmark-agnostic building block

BoleBench grades judges on races already run. AntePost closes the loop forward — an ante-post bet is placed before the field is even final; here, judgment is placed before anything is run — on any problem, from any benchmark or none.

  1. Agents propose. Given a live problem, agents draft candidate solutions — before anything is run.
  2. Humans propose too. The data we are collecting next: experts read the agent proposals, add their own, and make their own pre-execution call — the ante-post pick.
  3. Everything becomes blinded pairs. Agent vs agent, human vs agent — the same reality-resolved pairwise primitive, formulated before the outcome exists.
  4. A judge selects, ex ante. Another agent picks the side that is more promising and better aligned with research intuition. Execution then settles who was right — and every resolution becomes a new benchmark item.

Benchmark-agnostic by construction: it needs only a problem, proposals, and one eventual execution. Any pipeline that runs experiments can mint reality-resolved taste items.
